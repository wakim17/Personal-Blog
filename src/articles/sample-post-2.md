I was observing and counting the trees through a car window as they passed by under the moonlight. Six hundred sixty-six trees, to be precise. The 667th tree was just a tree stump, which was weird; I probably miscounted, but it was peculiar when there were no more trees to count after the stump.

Dad: *“FINALLY, through this town, we might make it to the wedding.”*

Mum: *"Let's just sleep here to pass the night. Instead make it without looking exhausted, and Sophie seems too tired.”*

Dad nods with a long sigh.

We went through the foggy town searching for a hotel or somewhere we could stay. The town consists of one restaurant, one supermarket, one barbershop, and six houses; the rest of the land is a massive field of dead grass. Among the dead grass, there was an old and gothic church with a shiny and clean bell, which was mysterious.

We parked and exited the car. I felt this strange cold breeze with the smell of sewage. I glanced at my parents. They were both smiling, and they looked very excited; at that moment I knew we were safe in this ghost town.

At the reception of the hotel, the silence continued. I was admiring the clock while my dad was trying to communicate with the zombie-like worker. I can hear the ticking of the clock as time passes; it gets louder and louder until the clock hits 12, the lights go out, the bell starts ringing, and a choir has started. A minute passes by, and the lights are suddenly illuminated. I looked behind where my parents are supposed to be; there was a path of blood on the floor that leads to a door behind the reception desk. Must I follow it or not? If the blood was my parents, why didn’t they scream when it was dark? I think I should wait for my parents to find me.

After thirty minutes of waiting, the lights started flashing, and the choir started singing, but this time, the bell was silent, and the choir came from the door. I walked a bit closer until I saw flashy purple eyes that blinked at me and disappeared into the darkness behind the door. I decided to follow the bloody path.

Through the door was a stairway, a very long circular stair made out of cobblestone; there was a deep hole of darkness and an oculus. It felt like the stairway to hell. I admired the oculus and realised that it viewed the sky and almost the moon; my focus was interrupted by the delightful sound of children harmonising a chorale. I kept walking down and following the blood as the tune became louder but sorrowful; I kept going but with a little fear.

I was too tired to keep moving; I laid my head on the cold, moist, blood-stained stone that smelt vegetal and a little citrussy. I think that's not blood as I started to drift to sleep; that's... Alcohol.

Silence, light, clouds, am I in heaven? Did I die? I don’t want it to end like this. *“CAN SOMEONE HEAR ME?"* Tears dropped from my face. *“I don't want to die; I haven't finished elementary school, I haven’t finished the treehouse with my dad, and I won’t make my sister’s wedding. "* I lay on the clouds, closed my flooded eyes, and drifted away.

Suddenly I woke up on the stairway with tears in my eyes. I didn't know what the time was, but at least I was happy it was a dream. *“Where did the stains go?”* I looked up and saw the oculus almost entirely covered by the moon. The choir interrupted my concentration with an irritating scream; it sounds like the kids being tortured. I also saw an orange light from a torch beneath me. I paced downstairs and saw an opened door where the irritating shout ended with a death rattle.

A Night of the Living Dead is what I saw in the room beneath: humans with missing limbs, half-floating demons and souls floating around the ceiling in a circular motion. A nun with a ghostly face, dry lips, and purple eyes instantly pushed me among the crowds of undead souls. I dropped to the floor in the middle of everyone. I felt the death stares from everyone; their half-burnt or missing faces looked confused. As soon as I wanted to say something, my dad, with a missing eye, his mouth stitched together, and his other eye white and his brain visible, waved at me and asked me to follow him using gestures.

He took me to a darker part of the room. I saw my mum – well, half of her. She was precisely cut vertically in half. The other half was probably the remainder of her soul; her organs were visible, at least what was left of them; her facial makeup was ruined, and she looked like she was crying. She gradually smiled at me and shouted, *“HAPPY 666th week"*, and proceeded with a comforting tone.

> *“A daughter is someone,*
>
> *you may always depend on* 
>
> *With a beautiful heart,*
>
> *Seek helpful advice.*
>
> *during a refreshing, nice walk.*
>
> *She will comfort you.*
>
> *when you're just feeling down,*
>
> *Understands you so well,*
>
> *every expression and frown.*
>
> *A daughter is someone.* 
>
> *you may joke with and laugh.* 
>
> *She is clearly your other half.”*

She looked at me while pointing a knife and asked me with her creepy half-smile, *“Will you be my other half?”* I started to slowly breathe and was frightened. My mum was trying to comfort me. I began to move back gradually; she knew I was going to decline her request, so with the last scream she jumped towards me with the knife pointing at me. I closed my eyes and covered my face with my arm. So I guess this is the end now; it is not a dream. My life was taken away from my mum.

The morning bell rang; seconds later, I opened my eyes to see my mum frozen in place with the knife, but she was human with a tear in her eye. I realised my mum had drug problems. I guess she wasn't herself. I sat down in the corner with regret; I just hoped my mum's last words were, *“I’m sorry, Sophia. "Now* I have to live this quiet life.
